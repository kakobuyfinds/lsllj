OrientDig shopping guide

A shortlist you can actually use.

Learning how to use an OrientDig spreadsheet starts with keeping the useful details together. A few complete records are easier to compare than a long collection of unexplained links.

  1. Choose one purpose

    Begin with a specific need: an everyday sneaker, a layering hoodie or a lightweight jacket. Filter OrientDig finds by product type before comparing unrelated styles.

  2. Keep the source attached

    Review product source links and save the original item ID. Record the seller, chosen color, size and the date you checked. OrientDig product links and original marketplace links may use different URL formats.

  3. Compare the same variant

    Compare product color options and measurements side by side. Confirm current product availability for the exact combination you want; a photograph of another variant is only a reference.

  4. Leave a reason for your decision

    Organize a product shortlist with a simple note: keep, revisit or remove. Record missing evidence, such as an unclear size chart or absent material description, before moving forward.

Buying field notes

Three checks before checkout.

Practical reading for clothing, footwear and accessories. No invented rankings or guaranteed outcomes.

01

Fit first: compare measurements, not labels

An OrientDig sizing guide is most useful when you have a reference garment. Lay a similar item flat, measure it, and compare the same points with the seller’s chart.

  • Shoes: check shoe size conversions against the model’s chart. Compare insole length when the measurement method is clear.
  • Tops: read a hoodie size chart for chest width, sleeve and length. Measure a T-shirt before buying to judge your preferred fit.
  • Layers: check jacket layering room with the clothing you expect to wear underneath.
  • Bottoms: compare pants rise and inseam. Check shorts waist measurements, including whether the waist is relaxed or stretched.

If the chart is incomplete, request missing measurements rather than guessing from a size letter.

02

Read photographs as evidence

OrientDig QC photos can help with visible checks when they are available. First confirm that the photos match the item, color and size you selected.

  • Compare listing and inspection photos from similar angles.
  • Inspect stitching in close-up photos around seams, hems, collars and attachment points.
  • Read warehouse QC photos for obvious marks, uneven alignment or a mismatched size label.
  • Ask for a clear detail shot when glare, distance or packaging hides an important area.

Lighting can change apparent color. Images alone do not establish authenticity, fiber content, comfort or long-term durability.

03

Plan the whole purchase

Estimate total purchase cost before treating an item price as your budget. Keep confirmed amounts separate from assumptions.

  • Check seller return conditions and any applicable service or payment charges.
  • Plan international shipping costs for your actual destination.
  • Compare packed parcel dimensions as well as weight; bulky packaging can affect freight.
  • Understand volumetric shipping weight using the chosen carrier’s current calculation and billing rules.

Allow for applicable import charges and check current provider information. A browsing guide cannot give a binding delivery quote.

The category notebook

Know what to compare.

Different product types need different questions. Use these checks when reviewing a listing.

A practical comparison checklist for your next shortlist
Category Record this Ask before deciding
Shoes Model, selected size, chart units and available length measurements. Does the chart describe the foot, the insole or another measurement?
Hoodies / T-Shirts Chest width, length, sleeve, composition and stated fit. Are the measurements for the exact size and taken flat?
Jackets Shoulders, chest, lining, closures and care information. Is there room for your intended layers?

Accessories Dimensions, closures, stated materials and care requirements. Do the photos show the clasp, edges and reverse side clearly?

What is an OrientDig spreadsheet?

An OrientDig spreadsheet is a product-discovery list associated with shopping through OrientDig. It may organize source links, categories, sizes and photographs. A row helps you research an item; it does not establish current stock, authenticity or quality.

Is this an official OrientDig website?

No. This is an independent editorial guide. It is not affiliated with OrientDig or the brands shown. Its browsing links open cafans.com; they do not place an OrientDig order.

Can QC photos prove authenticity?

No. QC photographs can help you inspect visible details such as color, stitching and the selected size. They cannot by themselves prove authenticity, material composition, durability or seller reliability.

How do I choose the right size?

Compare the listing’s measurements with a similar item you already own. Check the measurement method and units. For shoes, compare the model-specific size chart and any available insole measurements rather than relying only on a converted size label.

Does the product price include international shipping?

Do not assume it does. Confirm the item price, domestic delivery, service and payment costs, international freight and any applicable import charges separately. The packed parcel and destination affect the final total.

What should I do with a broken or outdated product link?

Keep the original item ID and mark the entry as unavailable until you can reopen the source. Do not assume a similar photograph identifies the same seller, variant or product.

Are the pictured products available on cafans.com?

The photographs are visual references, not a live stock feed or a promise of availability. Open the destination catalog and check the actual listing, seller, size, materials and current conditions.
